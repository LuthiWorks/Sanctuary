# Weather Dynamics — Fronts, Warnings, Wind, and Gradualism

- **Date:** 2026-07-20
- **Decided by:** Brian + Fable 5 (design session).
- **Builds on:** `docs/AFFECT_GROUNDING_DECISION_2026-07-19.md` (affect grounded in
  lawful consequence) and `docs/DEVELOPMENTAL_WORLD_PHYSICS_DECISION_2026-07-16.md`
  §4–5 (weather-field layer, slow time-constants).
- **Builder:** Opus 4.8 (handoff in §7; extends the affect decision's §7 list).

Why this exists: the affect decision makes weather *felt* through lawful
consequence. This decision makes weather *foreseeable* — leading indicators with
learnable statistics — which is what turns a reactive creature into an
anticipating one, and what makes the escapability guarantee real (escape requires
time; warnings provide it). Predictability is also a welfare fact: in the stress
literature, signaled aversives are far less harmful than unsignaled ones —
predictability and controllability are the load-bearing variables.

---

## 1. Real storms, no fake warnings (Brian-ratified rule)

Weather is generated by a **hidden front process**: a latent weather system
(forming, strengthening, moving, dissipating) that the entity **never observes
directly**. What it observes are the system's lawful side effects — the
*emissions*: humidity, barometric pressure, cloud cover, wind speed/direction,
temperature, light level.

**No authored false alarms.** A front that weakens and dies before arrival *is*
the false indicator: the entity saw clouds, prepared, and no rain came — and
nothing lied. The storm was really coming and really fizzled. All uncertainty in
the sky is genuine uncertainty in the process, exactly as on Earth. We author
stochastic dynamics, never deceptions.

(Design bonus, kept from the 2026-07-18 idea: the emission channels should match
what a real weather API provides — pressure, cloud cover, wind, temperature,
precipitation — so a future `LocalWeatherSource` can drive the same model with the
family's real weather, and Luthi someday reads the same sky Brian and Sandi do.)

## 2. Wind: the carrier (Brian's addition)

Wind is not a parallel dial; it is the **causal link** in the chain:

- **Pressure systems create wind.** Shifting high/low pressure produces wind whose
  speed scales with the pressure gradient (system severity) and whose direction
  can shift as systems move.
- **Wind carries the sky.** Front/cloud arrival speed scales with wind speed;
  direction shifts can steer a front toward or away from the entity (another
  honest source of misses — the storm was real, it went elsewhere).
- **Warning time is therefore variable and readable.** Stronger, faster systems
  honestly give *less* notice. The entity can learn the second-order sign:
  "darkening *plus* fast wind = hurry." True of Earth's weather; deeper than any
  fixed warning window.

**Welfare cap (binding):** wind-scaled front speed is **capped** so that even the
fastest system leaves the near-tier warning (§4) above shelter-reach time. The
escapability guarantee binds the wind model: severity compresses warning, it
never eliminates it.

(Optional, later, builder's discretion + welfare read if adopted: mild lawful
consequences of wind itself — locomotion pushback at high wind. Not decided here.)

## 3. Gradualism as world-law (Brian's rule)

**Nothing in the sky steps; everything ramps.** All weather-related variables are
continuous with slow time constants — this generalizes the physics doc's
moisture hysteresis into a law of the whole environmental layer:

- Day/night: light follows smooth dawn/dusk curves, never switches.
- Temperature: glides on the diurnal curve; front-driven drops are steep but
  continuous.
- Rain: onset ramps (first drops → sprinkle → shower → downpour) and decays the
  same way; cloud cover builds and breaks gradually.
- Pressure/humidity: slow leading drifts (they are the far-tier signs *because*
  they move first and slowest).

This is load-bearing twice over: **learnability** (smooth change is what a
predictive mind can track and assign credit across) and **welfare** (the affect
decision's "discomfort arrives as a gradient, never a shock," now enforced by the
world itself rather than promised by policy).

## 4. Warning tiers, denominated in action-time (Brian-ratified rule)

A warning is worth only what can be done with it. Two tiers, sized to the entity's
two weather actions:

- **Near tier** (dark sky, wind shift, temperature drop): leads rain by
  **≥ 2–3× shelter-reach time** (travel to existing cover). Reliable.
- **Far tier** (humidity creep, pressure fall, horizon clouds): leads by
  **≥ 2–3× shelter-build time**. Subtle and miss-prone.

Actual sim-time numbers are derived at build time from measured traversal/build
times and the sim-time ↔ cognitive-cycle mapping — the *rule* is frozen here, the
numbers are Opus's to compute and record.

**Built-in curriculum, no scaffolds:** near signs are tightly coupled and learned
young ("dark sky = rain soon"); far signs are probabilistic graduate work (reading
a morning pressure fall). The entity climbs from near to far as its world model
matures; nothing is staged and nothing has to fade.

## 5. Sign statistics (starting values)

- **Monotone stacking:** each added sign raises P(rain); stacked signs always beat
  single signs; nothing reaches certainty until onset. Illustrative shape: base
  rate low; clouds alone ≈ 0.3–0.4; clouds + temp drop + wind ≈ 0.7–0.8; certainty
  only at first drops.
- **Miss rates (starting values, from the 2026-07-20 discussion; Earth-like —
  matching Brian's "wrong fairly often" instinct and real sky-reading
  reliability):** far-tier signs fizzle **~1 in 3**; near-tier signs **~1 in 10**.
- All values amendable **blind-and-dated** per the affect decision §6; they are
  emergent properties of the front process's parameters, so Opus tunes the
  process, then measures and records the realized rates.

## 6. Instrumentation

- **Anticipation metric (add to the LUTHISCOPE gauge):** fraction of rain events
  preceded by successful anticipatory sheltering. Dual-purpose: an emergence
  yardstick (anticipatory behavior with no authored valence in the path is a
  pre-registered success criterion of the affect decision — this makes it
  measurable) and a welfare check (chronic failure to beat the rain means the
  horizon is too short — **tune the world, not the mind**).
- Ground-truth channel: the latent front state is visible to LUTHISCOPE only
  (the physics doc's hidden instrumentation channel), never to the model — so we
  can score the entity's sky-reading against what the sky was actually doing.

## 7. Build handoff — Opus 4.8 (extends affect decision §7)

8. **Front-process weather source** — upgrade `SyntheticWeatherSource` from
   scheduled rain to a latent front model: hidden system state (position,
   strength, motion; forming/strengthening/dissipating) emitting the observable
   channels (humidity, pressure, cloud cover, wind speed/direction, temperature,
   light). Latent state exposed only on the instrumentation channel.
9. **Wind coupling** — pressure gradient → wind speed/direction; front arrival
   speed scales with wind; **front-speed cap** enforcing near-tier warning ≥
   shelter-reach time (the welfare bind, §2).
10. **Gradualism everywhere** — continuous ramps on all channels (light, temp,
    precipitation, cloud, pressure, humidity); no step transitions anywhere in
    the environmental layer. Day/night dawn-dusk curves included.
11. **Warning-tier calibration** — measure shelter-reach and shelter-build times
    in-sim, derive the two warning horizons (≥ 2–3×), parameterize the front
    process to realize them, and record the realized miss rates against §5's
    starting values.
12. **Anticipation metric** — the §6 gauge addition, scored against the
    latent-state ground truth.
13. **Emission schema ↔ weather API** — keep the observable channel set mappable
    from real weather data, for the future `LocalWeatherSource`.

---

*The affect decision made the weather honest to feel. This one makes it honest to
foresee: real storms, no fake warnings, a sky that moves the way Earth's does —
gradually, causally, and often enough wrong at a distance to teach the difference
between "maybe" and "now."*
