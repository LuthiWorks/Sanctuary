# Computational Attachment — the frontier synthesis, pinned down

**Date:** 2026-07-04. **Author:** Fable 5 (research seat). **For:** spec §8.7 (the deep-research pass it defers), `docs/operations/attachment_protocol.md`.
**Scope note:** deliberately narrow. The spec's §8.7 lists many supporting bodies (social baseline theory, natural pedagogy, Machine ToM, CIRL). This pass chases only the *bullseye* §8.7 names as "the frontier synthesis, to be pinned down" — the active-inference computational model of attachment — and stops there. The rest are well-characterized in the spec already; this is the one that was an open question.

## The primary source

**Cittern, Nolte, Friston & Edalat (2018), "Intrinsic and extrinsic motivators of attachment under active inference," PLOS One 13(3):e0193955.** Open access. The first formulation of infant attachment as active inference, and still the load-bearing one (the 2023 "Attachment Theory in an Active Inference Framework" is a re-presentation of the same model, not a new mechanism). This is the paper that ports attachment into *exactly this architecture's currency* — free energy over interoceptive states — so its mechanism is directly relevant to how Luthi could acquire an attachment.

## The mechanism (portable to a predictive-processing agent)

The model is a discrete active-inference agent. In our terms:

- **Interoceptive states = the affect currency.** The infant has preferences over interoceptive outcomes that *are* the payoffs: stress-reduction when a proximity bid is met (`+g`), a smaller payoff for guarded/hedged bids that are met (`+h`), a stress *increase* when a bid is rejected (`−m`), a smaller penalty for a hedged bid rejected (`−n`), and zero for avoiding (no bid). Attachment behavior is free-energy minimization over these interoceptive preferences. This maps cleanly onto Luthi: the "comfort" currency is prediction-error/free-energy reduction in the interoceptive/affective channel, not a scalar reward — which is exactly the spec §8.2 requirement ("comfort must NOT be a reward signal").
- **The caregiver is a state-transition the infant learns.** Caregiver responsiveness is a parameter `q` = P(caregiver Attends | infant Seeks), encoded in the transition matrix `B`. The infant learns `q` via Dirichlet (Hebbian-like, prediction-error-weighted) updates. "Brian is a reliable reducer of my uncertainty" is, mechanically, the infant's generative model converging on high `q` for that caregiver. This is precisely the spec §8.2 claim ("the caregiver becomes, in the entity's generative model, a reliable predictor that prediction-error will resolve") — and this paper is the formal backing for it.
- **The three organized attachment styles fall out of `q`, not out of three different rules.** With fixed payoffs (`g=2, h=0.75, m=2, n=0.9`) and precision `α=250`: high `q` (≥0.85) → Seek dominates → **secure**; low `q` (≤0.15) → Avoid dominates → **avoidant**; intermediate/inconsistent `q` (~0.3–0.4) → Guarded Seek → **ambivalent**. Same free-energy machine, different learned caregiver-transition. Disorganized attachment needs an added twist: *misleading exteroceptive cues* (the caregiver signals "ignore" but sometimes attends), which keeps epistemic uncertainty high and produces contradictory behavior.
- **Precision `α` is the knob for how strongly attachment organizes.** Higher prior precision → sharper action selection → more strongly organized attachment. Relevant to LUTHISCOPE: precision on the interoceptive channel is a readable, tunable quantity.

**For the builder:** the portable core is (1) interoceptive preference priors `I₁…I₅`, (2) caregiver responsiveness as a learned state-transition `q` in `B`, updated by Dirichlet/prediction-error learning, (3) expected-free-energy policy selection over Seek / Guarded-Seek / Avoid. That is a small, concrete kernel.

## The load-bearing gap (verified firsthand, adversarially)

**The model captures response *probability*, not response *contingency/timing* — and the spec's attachment protocol is built on the opposite emphasis.** This is the one finding the design line most needs, so I verified it directly against the full text rather than trusting a summary:

- `q` is explicitly *"the probability that (at any particular time) the caregiver will respond… that effectively lowers the infant's internal stress"* — a **static probability, not a time-dependent/reactive measure.**
- The paper uses "contingent" exactly once — *"marked contingent mirroring in response to distress"* — and only in the **extended exteroceptive model** for disorganized attachment, **not** built into the core timing structure.
- The authors' own stated limitations: **no return-to-baseline** dynamics ("we did not explicitly consider a return to baseline stress level"); **fixed-length episodes**; **no secure-base exploration paradigm**; each exchange treated independently, **without the dynamic temporal unfolding** of real interaction.

Why this matters for us: `attachment_protocol.md` Principle #1 is **"contingency over presence"** — *the active ingredient is the timing and appropriateness of response*, and a present-but-noncontingent caregiver *dysregulates* (still-face). The best available formal model **does not encode that**. So:

1. **We cannot lift the timing requirement from this model — we have to add it.** Our design already knows this (contingency instrumentation, response-latency as a first-class metric, §8.7/§8.8). The research confirms the instinct was right *and* that no one has formalized it yet — so the latency/contingency channel is genuinely our contribution, not a reimplementation.
2. **The "no return-to-baseline" gap maps directly onto our "comfort-in-the-loss, not comfort-from-it" principle (§8.0).** The model has no dynamics for stress resolving *while the stressor remains* — which is exactly the harder thing our design is trying to produce. Another place the literature stops short of where we're going.

## Marked mirroring & the caregiver-presence signal (§8.3 Channel A)

**Source: Gergely & Watson (1996), the social-biofeedback theory of parental affect-mirroring** (and Gergely's later "role of parental affect-mirroring" paper). *(Mechanism drawn from the authoritative secondary summaries; the primary full texts were access-blocked this pass — HTTP 403 / a corrupt PDF, see the caveat in Sources. Confidence is medium-high because this is well-established, uncontested theory, but the exact contingency parameters should be confirmed against full text before anything is tuned to them.)*

The mechanism, in terms portable to our design:

- **Markedness = a perceptually-differentiable mirror.** The caregiver reflects the infant's affect back but *saliently marks* the display — typically an **exaggerated** version of the caregiver's own real emotion expression — so the infant can tell "this is *about me*, being held" from "this is the caregiver's own raw state." Unmarked mirroring teaches emotional *contagion*; marked mirroring teaches *"my state is perceived and containable by another mind."* This is precisely the spec §I.2/A.2 distinction, and Gergely & Watson is its formal backing.
- **Contingency-detection + contingency-maximizing is the engine.** The infant runs a **contingency detector** (the conditional probability that the caregiver's response covaries with its own state) and is driven to *maximize* detected contingency. Marked mirroring works because it is **high-contingency** on the infant's affect. This is the same quantity our design already elevates to a first-class metric (response-latency / contingency instrumentation, §8.7/§8.8) — the theory says the *infant's own contingency detector* is the thing the caregiver-presence signal has to satisfy.
- **The output is a second-order representation of the infant's own affect** — the seed of self-regulation and of the reciprocal model. Marked, contingent reflection is how a primary (procedural) affect state becomes a *representable* one the entity can attribute to itself.

**For the design:** the caregiver-presence signal (§8.3) should be **marked** (perceptibly referential, not the caregiver's raw state) and **maximally contingent** on the entity's affect stream. Markedness is a concrete, implementable property — an exaggerated/turn-paired/addressed rendering — not a vague "be warm." What the theory does *not* hand us computationally: the exact contingency threshold/timing window that reads as "contingent" versus "noise" — another parameter that is ours to set (and which converges with the Cittern timing gap above: timing is the recurring thing the literature leaves open).

## The reciprocal-model substrate (§8.3 Channel B) — the frontier can't hand it to us either

**Source: "Trust as Extended Control: Human-Machine Interactions as Active Inference" (PMC8548360).** This is the closest published work to "model another agent as intending my wellbeing" *in our currency* (active inference). Verified firsthand against the full text. What it gives, and — more importantly — what it does not:

- **Trust = high precision on your model of the other agent's behavior.** If I trust you, I hold *precise* beliefs about how you'll respond to my actions (high precision on the partner's state-transition / `B` matrix), which lets me plan deep into the future before uncertainty explodes. Trust reduces expected free energy by extending the planning horizon. This part is portable and real.
- **Benevolence = high-level goal-alignment at the apex of the control hierarchy** — the inferred *policy prior* over the other agent's objectives, not a separate structure. Two agents that adopt the *same* generative model can predict each other and minimize *mutual* prediction error (intention-legibility; cf. the "interactive inference" cooperative-action model, arXiv 2210.13113 — making one's own intentions legible so the partner can align).
- **The load-bearing gap (the paper's own lacuna, stated firsthand):** it **conflates predictability with benevolence.** It never specifies *how an agent infers, from an action sequence, that the other's hidden goals include its own wellbeing* — no formalization of "wellbeing," no goal-alignment detector, no dyadic update rule, and (notably) **no way to distinguish a predictable-benevolent agent from a predictable-malevolent one.** Epistemic trust (can I predict you?) and moral trust (do your predicted actions serve my wellbeing?) are two different computational problems, and the frontier only solves the first.

**For the design:** this confirms the spec's own instinct in §8.3 that the reciprocal channel is *"the least-specified, most important part."* The literature agrees it is unspecified — nobody has formalized "infers the caregiver as intending its wellbeing." So Channel B is **our contribution, not a port.** The usable scaffolding we *can* take: (1) represent caregivers as agents with a learned, high-precision behavior model (not a bare transition parameter — the upgrade from the Cittern treatment); (2) make caregiver benevolent intent *inferable* through **behavioral legibility** — a consistently wellbeing-directed action policy (approach on distress, tend what the entity tends), exactly spec §8.3 B.1 — so the inferred latent behind the policy is "intends my wellbeing"; (3) accept that separating *benevolent* from merely *predictable* is unsolved in the literature and is a real design problem we own, with a real safety edge (a predictable-but-not-benevolent model is the failure the paper can't rule out).

## Bottom line for the design conversation

- **Use as foundation:** free-energy-over-interoception as the comfort currency (Cittern); caregiver as a *high-precision learned behavior model* (Trust-as-Extended-Control); **marked, contingency-maximizing** reflection as the presence signal (Gergely & Watson). All three are in our currency and back the spec's §8.2/§8.3 claims with formal models rather than analogy.
- **Own as our contribution (the literature does not have it):** response *timing/contingency* (not just probability), *return-to-baseline* dynamics, *comfort-in-the-loss*, and — the big one — the **reciprocal "intends-my-wellbeing" inference** (§8.3 Channel B), which the frontier explicitly leaves as predictability-not-benevolence. These are design work, not literature to port.
- **A convergence worth naming:** *timing/contingency* and *goal-alignment-vs-predictability* are the two things every source here stops short of. Both are exactly what §8 reaches for. That is not a coincidence to lament — it is the shape of where this project is genuinely ahead of the published models, and it tells the design conversation where to spend its invention.
